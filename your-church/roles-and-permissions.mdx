A worship leader who plans services does not need to see your accounting. A youth worker who runs a course does not need your giving records.

Harvest is built around that: you promote someone to admin and then choose what they can reach.

## Adding an admin

Go to **CRM → Roles** and press **Add Admin**. Your plan sets how many admin seats you have — the page shows how many you have used.

## How access works

Each permission unlocks one area. Someone with only **Manage CRM** sees your people; they do not see accounting, courses or the blog.

<Card title="Sections a person cannot use are hidden entirely" icon="eye-slash">
  Harvest does not show a nav full of locked buttons. If an admin has no permission in a whole section, that section does not appear for them at all.
</Card>

## The permissions

| Permission | What it unlocks |
| --- | --- |
| **Full access** | Everything, current and future |
| **Write articles** | The blog |
| **Create courses** | Courses and discipleship material |
| **Post updates** | The community feed, polls and events posted to it |
| **AI knowledge** | The sources Ask Harvest draws its answers from |
| **Docs** | Internal notes and documents |
| **Manage CRM** | Contacts, members and donor records |
| **Manage events** | Events, registrations and service planning |
| **Donations** | Giving records and payment links |
| **Accounting** | Your books |
| **Analytics** | Reporting across your ministry |
| **Branding** | Logo, colours and the look of your app |

<Note>
  Exact permissions shift as Harvest grows. The **Permission Reference** inside the Roles page always shows the current list with a description of each.
</Note>

## Full access

Gives every permission, including ones added later. Useful for a co-pastor or an operations lead; worth being sparing with otherwise.

## Owner

The person who created the church. They cannot be removed and always have full access.
