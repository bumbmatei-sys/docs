---
title: "Recording a gift"
description: "One entry updates your dashboard, accounting, the member's history and their statement."
---

Because gifts arrive in your own payment account, Harvest only knows about them once you record them. This is the routine that keeps everything correct.

## How to record one

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the contact">
    Go to **CRM**, find the person and open their record.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add a donation">
    Press **Add Activity**, choose **Donation**, and enter the amount and date.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Save">
    That is it. One entry, four places updated.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What one entry updates

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Your dashboard" icon="chart-line">
    The giving total on your overview moves immediately.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Accounting" icon="receipt">
    An invoice is created automatically. You do not enter it twice.
  </Card>

  <Card title="The member's own history" icon="user">
    They see the gift in their account, with a receipt they can download.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Their giving statement" icon="file-invoice">
    It lands in the right year automatically.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## The email matters more than you think

<Warning>
  A gift is matched to a member **by their email address**. If you record a gift against a contact with no email, the gift still counts on your dashboard and in your accounting — but **the member will never see it** in their own account, and it will not appear on their statement.

  Harvest tells you this on screen before you save. It is not a silent failure, but it is easy to click past.
</Warning>

If you are recording a gift for someone who will want a year-end statement, make sure their contact has the email address they use to sign in.

## A weekly rhythm works best

Most churches set aside a few minutes once a week: open your payment account, open Harvest side by side, and record what came in.

<Tip>
  Doing it weekly rather than monthly keeps your dashboard honest, and it means a member asking "did my gift come through?" gets a real answer.
</Tip>

## Gifts you cannot attribute

Sometimes money arrives with no name you recognise. Record it against the contact if you can identify them later; if you never can, it is still real income for your accounting even though no member sees it.
